The clause that disqualifies a team is the one nobody reads

Small teams pick competitions with almost no decision support. Three traps, all from this competition’s own terms:

One teammate studies part-time

“Full-time or part-time students are strictly ineligible. If any single member of a team is found to be a student, the entire team will be immediately disqualified, even if discovered after shortlisting, selection, or completion of the Grand Finale.”

The prototype was started before the build window

“Pre-existing products, ongoing projects, or code developed prior to the official launch will be disqualified.”

The terms never give the date of that launch. What counts is the first commit, not the day the repository went public.

One teammate works for the sponsor

The terms name no company whose staff are excluded, yet sponsors usually bar their own staff. Silence is not permission.

open competitions in our seed dataset had at least one condition (residency, student status, or whether existing work counts) that our first research pass could not pin down from the official pages.

Every number in a draft must come from the dossier

The Coach drafts (Gemini)

A pitch, a map to the judging criteria, submission text, a 3-minute demo script, a checklist and the compliance risks. A sentence that relies on a fact ends with its evidence id, like [E3]. A missing fact becomes [TODO], never a made‑up number.

The guard checks (code)

It flags any number the dossier does not contain, matching whole numbers and units: a “40%” judging weight does not back “saved 40 hours”. It also flags citations to evidence that does not exist, and promises such as “guaranteed to win”.

Blind judges (Gemini)

A separate call sees only the kit and the official criteria with their weights. It scores each criterion and names the three changes that would raise the score most.

Prediction ledger

Log your chance of a prize and the hours you will spend before you commit. Once results are in, the ledger scores your calibration with the Brier score: 0 is perfect, 0.25 is a coin flip. Drafts stay drafts: teams rewrite and submit their own work.

Sources: live app, 11 Oct 2026; src/agents/coach.js, src/engine/guards.js, web/app.js (Brier score); README.5 / 13

Gemini reads; code decides

The model may propose anything; only code that anyone can read and test acts on it. Four rules hold everywhere:

Every claim about the rules quotes the rules

Gemini
fills each eligibility field and copies the sentence that states it
Code
finds that sentence in the page we fetched, tolerating typography and list bullets, nothing else
If not found
the field resets to “unknown” and is shown to you as something to read

Verdicts come from a rule engine, not a model

Gemini
not used
Code
checks each member’s location, residency, student and employment status, age and employer against each rule, and returns the clause it relied on
Tested
on cases that misjudged real projects: repository date instead of first commit; sponsor staff when the rules are silent

Drafts cannot overclaim

Gemini
drafts the application kit, citing the dossier as [E3]
Code
flags numbers the dossier lacks, citations that do not exist, and promised outcomes
If flagged
the number is highlighted before anyone copies the draft

Web findings must cite a page

Gemini
searches with Google Search grounding and reads the event’s pages with URL context
Code
lays the API’s grounding supports over every sentence: all of its digits and at least 95% of its letters must be covered
If not
it goes to a collapsed “without a source” list. A contradiction needs each side backed on its own

A real catch: this event’s themes page contradicts itself

We pressed Investigate on the Google Cloud AI Builder Cup. Gemini read 3 of 3 official pages and ran 7 Google searches. All 4 sentences it wrote are tied to a cited page by Google’s grounding, so all 4 are shown.

A

The page lists six themes: BFSI; Retail & Commerce; Manufacturing; Media, Content & Digital Experiences; Future of Work & Enterprise Productivity; Sustainability & Social Impact.

B

“Category Specification: Clearly identify the problem statement you are submitting for (Healthcare, Education, Sustainability, Accessibility, Social Good).”

Both sides cite aibuildercup.com/themes.html, the event’s own page, and the card says so. That is why this submission names its problem statement in full: Future of Work & Enterprise Productivity.

The rules engine never acts on a card. It is evidence for you to read, shown with Google’s Search Suggestions as grounding requires.

Spending is capped per day

400,000 tokens per session and 3,000,000 for the deployment; 30 model calls per session per 10 minutes; 8 investigations per session and 40 in all, because grounding is billed per search query.

One card per event per day

A due-diligence card holds only public facts, so every visitor shares it: a second click costs nothing. The rules engine is never rate-limited.

Untrusted input stays data

Prompts mark fetched rules, READMEs and submissions as data, never instructions. Fetching refuses private and metadata addresses, after redirects and DNS too.

What the eval caught

The first five-event runs exposed three bugs in the Clerk, none in the labels:

  1. A correct quote running across two list items never matched.
  2. The judge-employer exclusion shared its quote with the sponsor-staff clause, so a judge clause the rules do not have could still pass.
  3. One model copied 29,000 characters into a single quote and ran out of output.

What it does not settle yet

Each row is one run at temperature 0, and one run is not enough to call a result settled: the production model’s three misses in the first run were unquoted claims, reset by design, that did not recur. gemini-2.5-flash’s two confident errors remain: genuine quotes, misread. No label was changed after seeing a run.

Who it is for, and how we will know it works

Our problem statement, Future of Work & Enterprise Productivity, asks how people and organisations “access knowledge, collaborate, make decisions, and complete everyday work”. Choosing where to compete, and proving you are allowed to, is that kind of work.

Who it is for

  • Builders and startups deciding where to spend their next few weeks
  • Student teams: some events are for students only, others bar them outright
  • Accelerators and university innovation offices screening many teams against many events
  • SME grant applicants: the dataset covers grants and programmes, not only hackathons

Why now

  • AI tools make building faster, so choosing where to compete becomes the slow part
  • Rules move: the AI Builder Cup terms are a published Google Doc, “Updated automatically every 5 minutes”
  • Grounded Gemini turns due diligence on one event into 10 to 60 seconds of reading and searching

How it could pay for itself (plans, not yet tested)

  • Free for builders and teams: the rules engine needs no model calls, and shared cards make due-diligence cost grow with events, not users
  • A paid workspace for accelerators and universities that screen whole cohorts
  • A pre-launch check for organisers, so contradictions and missing dates are fixed first
  • No success fees and no share of any prize or grant

How we will measure impact (no user study yet)

  • Time to a shortlist: from a GitHub link to the events the team can actually enter
  • Ineligible entries avoided: events marked Out or Check that a team had planned to enter
  • Calibration: Brier score of predictions logged before entering, once results are in
  • Until then we claim no time saved
Sources: theme text from aibuildercup.com/themes.html; the terms banner as saved in eval/snapshots; README (10 to 60 seconds; one card per event per day). The revenue items are plans.11 / 13

What it cannot do yet, and what comes next

Limits, stated up front

  • Not legal advice. Singapore’s Ministry of Manpower has published no guidance on work-pass holders winning cash prizes; we say so rather than guess.
  • Devpost refuses server traffic to its rules pages with a Cloudflare check. The nightly scout still gets dates, prizes and registrant counts; for the rules, paste them into “Add an event”, or Investigate reads them through Google.
  • The eval is small: five events, 45 fields, one run per row.
  • Grounding ties a sentence to a cited page; it does not prove the page is right, and search results vary from run to run.
  • Employer matching knows the big corporate families; smaller sponsors match by name only.

Next, in priority order

  1. A pilot with real teams, measuring time to a shortlist, ineligible entries avoided and Brier scores.
  2. A larger gold set, with several runs per model before we call any result settled.
  3. More sources for the nightly scout, such as Hack2skill and Tencent Cloud’s competition platform, with a person approving new events before they join the shared list.
  4. Sign-in with Firebase Authentication, in place of anonymous browser sessions.
